## Rules that apply to every workflow

- Never invent citations, DOI values, data, estimates, uncertainty, or journal
  rules.
- Preserve population, geography, period, units, variables, equations, model,
  and claim type.
- Distinguish description, prediction, association, causal identification,
  structural estimation, and simulation.
- Verify every factual claim, quotation, citation, and calculation against an
  authoritative source or executed artifact.
- Do not submit restricted, confidential, embargoed, proprietary, or personal
  data to an unapproved model or service.
- Retain null results, limitations, and contradictory evidence.
- Keep a reviewable change log and follow current disclosure and authorship
  rules.

## The 17 workflow categories

1. Chinese draft to English economics prose: preserve technical meaning and
   flag ambiguous terminology instead of guessing.
2. English economics prose to a Chinese reading copy: create a reading aid,
   preserve claim strength, and keep the English source authoritative.
3. Chinese academic rewriting: organize the question, setting, identification,
   result, and limitation without adding evidence.
4. Careful condensation: remove repetition while retaining design, estimates,
   uncertainty, citations, and meaningful null results.
5. Evidence-bounded expansion: clarify logic already supported by supplied
   material and list gaps that require research.
6. English economics editing: improve clarity while preserving equations,
   citation keys, numerical values, and claim strength.
7. Chinese economics editing: improve formal prose without manufacturing
   novelty, mechanisms, or policy effectiveness.
8. Logic and identification review: map each claim to evidence, assumptions,
   threats, defenses, and unresolved gaps before polishing language.
9. Removing formulaic AI style from LaTeX prose: preserve commands, labels,
   references, tables, and disclosures.
10. Removing formulaic AI style from Word prose: remove generic wording while
    preserving numbers, units, citations, and qualifications.
11. Research-design figures: show units, timing, comparison, assignment, and
    identification without implying unsupported causal paths.
12. Empirical chart selection: choose charts from the inferential task and
    label axes, units, uncertainty, weights, and sample restrictions.
13. Figure titles: name the quantity, comparison, period, and geography without
    announcing a stronger result than the figure supports.
14. Table titles: distinguish descriptive statistics, estimates, robustness,
    heterogeneity, and forecast evaluation.
15. Empirical-results analysis: report direction, magnitude, units,
    uncertainty, stability, and claim type from supplied results only.
16. Reviewer simulation: separate design-level, analytical, reproducibility,
    and presentation concerns; treat the output as diagnostic, not peer review.
17. Model and tool selection: choose tools by task, source authority, privacy,
    reproducibility, and human verification requirements.

## Citation and result checks

Open every cited source. Confirm author, title, publisher or venue, date, DOI
when applicable, and the exact claim supported. Check every result against the
table cell, source row, or executed artifact. Recheck signs, units, samples,
reference groups, uncertainty, and revision policy.

## Identification review

Naming DID, IV, RDD, an event study, a panel model, a forecast, or a simulation
does not prove its assumptions. Record the estimand, source of variation,
comparison, timing, measurement, selection risks, and unresolved threats. Do
not rewrite predictive accuracy or simulated behavior as causal evidence.

## Disclosure and privacy

Check current journal, employer, funder, institution, and data-provider rules.
Preserve required AI-use disclosures. Record what tool received what authorized
input, what it produced, how a human verified it, and what was rejected.
